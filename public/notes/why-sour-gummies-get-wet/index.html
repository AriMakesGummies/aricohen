Why sour-coated gummies get wet, and how to fix it

Do you ever wonder why you open a bag of gummies and they’re wet?

The answer comes down to the chemistry.

When gummies are demolded they are often coated in a sour sugar mix. This is especially true of cannabis gummies, because the sour taste acts as another masking agent for the bitter input.

This mix is usually made up of regular sugar and some kind of acid (almost always citric). And this is where we find the problem.

There are two kinds of citric acid commonly used for gummies: regular and encapsulated.

Citric acid, in most forms we would use, is anhydrous and hygroscopic. In technical terms, this means it both contains no water and it is highly likely to draw out or attract moisture from its surroundings. In basic terms, it means this acts like a water magnet for anything with even a little moisture nearby.

You’ve got gummies that, despite being cured properly to mitigate mold from water content, are coated in hygroscopic powder and then sealed in a Mylar bag that might as well be a sauna for humidity. So despite the water activity being technically low, thirsty little citric acid is so parched and desperate for water it will do anything to get just a few drops. It has no remorse for the damp destruction it is about to cause.

That bag sits on a shelf for a few months, and by the time it is finally opened, those gummies are wetter than my eyes when Bing Bong jumps off the rocket wagon in Inside Out. Silly, selfless Bing Bong.

It’s an easy fix. Encapsulated citric acid takes away all these worries. The acid is coated in a small layer of fat, which keeps it from pulling in water. It acts like a buffer and it is extremely stable. It is a special order and certainly pricier, and often takes a little more work to source than just clicking on Amazon.

But that’s the chemistry behind it!
